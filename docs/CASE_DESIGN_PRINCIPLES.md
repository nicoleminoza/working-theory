# Working Theory — Case Design Principles

## Purpose

These principles govern how Working Theory cases are written, structured, designed, and evaluated.

They are cross-case rules, not case-specific content specs.

## 1. The unit is the judgment, not the project

A case should not answer:

> What did Nicole work on?

It should answer:

> What did Nicole have to decide?

Project complexity matters only when it changes the decision.

Do not preserve detail merely because it proves the work was large or difficult.

## 2. Show judgment in motion

The reader should be able to watch the reasoning change.

A useful hidden grammar is:

> evidence → tension → judgment → reality pushes back → model changes → action → proof → earned learning

Do not expose this as a visible framework.

Do not add labels such as Situation, Task, Action, Result, Competency, or What this demonstrates.

The case should feel editorial, not instructional.

## 3. Every case needs a distinct job in the set

Consistency means consistent quality of thinking, not identical structure.

Do not force the cases into the same choreography.

For the current set:
- Problem 01 owns platform adoption and governance
- Problem 02 owns commercial judgment and business-model migration
- Problem 03 owns positioning and narrative

A supporting idea may appear in another case, but should not become its central conflict.

## 4. Make the business judgment legible before the execution volume

The reader should understand the call before being asked to absorb the work it created.

A strong case usually makes clear:
- what changed
- what was at risk
- what alternatives existed
- what Nicole believed
- what she chose to do about it

Then show the system required to execute the decision.

Do not lead with a long list of activities.

## 5. Preserve real alternatives

Senior judgment is easier to see when the reader understands what else could have happened.

Where historically true, show the easier, cheaper, safer, politically simpler, or locally optimal alternative.

Do not manufacture false choices.

Do not make the eventual decision look inevitable in hindsight.

## 6. Reality should push back

A strong Working Theory case includes evidence that complicates the initial plan.

Examples may include:
- partner resistance
- missing product capability
- team-level incentives
- technical readiness
- customer risk
- performance constraints
- new evidence

The important question is:

> What changed in Nicole’s model after reality pushed back?

Do not force a failure beat where none existed.

## 7. Revision is stronger than certainty

Working Theory is built around points of view that are strong enough to act on and open enough to revise.

A case becomes more credible when Nicole can say:
- what she held
- what she changed
- why the new evidence mattered

Do not frame every adjustment as a reversal.

Sometimes the strategy stays and the execution changes.

## 8. Be explicit about ownership

Ownership boundaries should be visible when they matter.

Use clear distinctions such as:
- “My GM made the call…”
- “I made the business case…”
- “Engineering owned the architecture…”
- “I specified the minimum behavior…”
- “I owned the adoption strategy…”

Never imply ownership of Engineering implementation, Finance decisions, Legal work, contracts, negotiations, or executive decisions unless that ownership is historically accurate.

Precision builds trust.

## 9. Product Marketing should be visible in the judgment, not in a checklist

Do not try to prove PMM by adding:
- launch-plan checklists
- channel inventories
- generic stakeholder lists
- deliverable inventories
- competency labels

Show PMM judgment through decisions such as:
- what the product should mean
- why a customer should care
- how proof changes by audience
- what Product must make possible
- how rollout should be sequenced
- what evidence should test the promise
- when business logic and customer logic differ

## 10. Technical detail must serve the business argument

Technical specificity is valuable when it explains why the decision was hard or what had to become possible.

Keep details such as telemetry, shared engines, release cadence, interoperability, usage definition, and performance only when they help the reader understand the business judgment.

Do not let the case become a technical architecture explainer.

## 11. Audience translation should reveal different incentives

Different audience messages are useful only when the audiences genuinely need different proof or logic.

Do not repeat the same idea under multiple audience labels.

Ask:

> What does this audience need to believe for the strategy to work?

Use audience sections selectively.

Problem 01 does not need a conventional messaging matrix.

Problem 02 uses them selectively.

Problem 03 needs them strongly.

## 12. A customer reason must stand on its own

Internal business logic is not automatically customer value.

Where relevant, distinguish:
- why the company needs a change
- why the customer should accept or value it

Do not disguise an internal cost-saving motive as customer benefit.

The customer-facing reason must be credible on its own.

## 13. Rollout decisions should reveal risk judgment

Keep rollout details when they show a real choice about timing, sequencing, recoverability, segmentation, technical readiness, customer surprise, or partner impact.

Remove routine execution details that merely show competent program management.

A useful test:

> Does this detail reveal a new decision or consequence?

If not, cut or subordinate it.

## 14. Proof should test the claim

A case’s evidence should answer the question the case raised.

Examples:
- if the position promises membership value, measure retention
- if the business-model change promises economic sustainability, show savings and partner continuity
- if the platform strategy promises adoption, show migration and product impact

Do not add unrelated metrics because they are impressive.

Do not recap all metrics after the conclusion.

## 15. End on the earned theory

Each case ends with:
1. the theory
2. one supporting line if needed

Then stop.

Do not append Method, Demonstrates, Skills, Competencies, a metric recap, or a résumé-style summary.

Navigation sits outside the editorial conclusion.

## 16. The theory should operate one level above the implementation

A theory should not merely restate the mechanism.

Weak:

> We needed telemetry.

Stronger:

> A business model only works if the product can measure what it values.

The theory should capture the transferable judgment earned by the case while still feeling specific enough to have come from it.

## 17. Do not optimize each case against the whole job description

Working Theory is not the entire application package.

Do not force every role requirement into every case.

Use the cases to show judgment deeply.

Use the résumé, application note, Make It Land, and interview to cover career scale, literal role requirements, domain gaps, conventional SaaS experience, current practice, and other proof.

## 18. Reading one case is a valid complete visit

Never design the experience to imply that the visitor must finish all cases.

No progress bars, case counts, “1 of 3,” checkmarks, locks, recommended next case, or forced sequence.

The visitor chooses based on the problem that interests them.

## 19. Earned state represents knowledge, not completion

A theory is earned only when the final learning enters view.

The home page remembers:

> what the visitor now knows

not:

> what the visitor completed

Unread rows remain visually unchanged.

Earned rows receive only a quiet annotation.

## 20. Cases can use different visual mechanics

Use interaction only when it improves comprehension.

Do not add interaction for consistency.

For example:
- Problem 01 may work best as a largely static editorial narrative
- Problem 02 may use an interactive mechanism to explain the economic mismatch
- Problem 03 may use motion to show reframing or consequences

The visual device must serve the judgment.

## 21. Editorial tone

Visitor-facing copy should be direct, specific, plain, sayable aloud, experienced, and unsentimental.

Avoid consultant language, vague strategy language, AI-generic phrasing, stacked abstractions, and em dashes.

Prefer concrete verbs and nouns.

Avoid words and phrases such as leverage, unlock, ecosystem, operating system, strategic thesis, stakeholder contract, holistic, align, and north star.

## 22. Visual system

Working Theory should feel like:

> **an excellent editorial designer made a strategy product**

Design system:
- Newsreader display
- Hanken Grotesk body
- Spline Sans Mono labels
- paper `#FAF6F1`
- paper-2 `#F2EADF`
- ink `#241F1D`
- plum `#6B2C41`
- terracotta `#C07A44`
- rule `#E6DCCF`

Use flat bands, hairlines, editorial spacing, and strong type hierarchy.

Avoid SaaS cards, dashboards, icons, decorative frameworks, and progress UI.

Working Theory is not a separate personal brand.

## 23. Final case test

Before locking a case, ask:

> **Can I watch the business judgment happen?**

Then:

> **Does this case prove something the other cases do not?**

Then:

> **Is every remaining detail necessary to understand the judgment, the pushback, the action, or the proof?**

If the answer to all three is yes, stop polishing.
